Gui Zhi Jia Hou Po Xing Zi Tang (桂枝加厚朴杏子汤): Ingredients, Uses & Original Text

Gui Zhi Jia Hou Po Xing Zi Tang — 桂枝加厚朴杏子汤, the Cinnamon Twig Decoction with Magnolia Bark and Apricot Kernel — is a classical Chinese herbal formula of 7 herbs from the Shang Han Lun. It belongs to the Taiyang (Greater Yang) stage and is aimed at one pattern: taiyang wind-strike with failure of lung qi to descend.

Ingredients of Gui Zhi Jia Hou Po Xing Zi Tang

  • Gui Zhi (cinnamon twig) — 9g · bark removed
  • Bai Shao (white peony root) — 9g
  • Zhi Gan Cao (honey-fried licorice) — 6g
  • Sheng Jiang (fresh ginger) — 9g · sliced
  • Da Zao (jujube) — 12 pieces · split
  • Hou Po (magnolia bark) — 6g · dry-fried with ginger juice
  • Xing Ren (apricot kernel) — 6g · skin and tip removed

Doses are typical modern textbook amounts for one day's decoction, shown for study. They are not a prescription.

What Gui Zhi Jia Hou Po Xing Zi Tang is used for

Classical pattern: Taiyang wind-strike with failure of lung qi to descend.

Typical pulse: floating, moderate pulse.

Modern clinical uses: Common cold with sweating accompanied by wheezing and cough.

The original line

When a chronic wheezing patient develops an acute exterior attack, Gui Zhi Tang with Hou Po and Xing Zi is optimal.

喘家作，桂枝汤加厚朴杏子佳。

— Shang Han Lun, line 18

How to prepare Gui Zhi Jia Hou Po Xing Zi Tang

Decoct with water, take warm, and induce a gentle sweat.

Cautions

Contraindicated if sweating is completely absent.

Questions about Gui Zhi Jia Hou Po Xing Zi Tang

What is Gui Zhi Jia Hou Po Xing Zi Tang used for?

In classical terms Gui Zhi Jia Hou Po Xing Zi Tang treats taiyang wind-strike with failure of lung qi to descend. Modern references apply it to common cold with sweating accompanied by wheezing and cough. It should be chosen by pattern, by a qualified practitioner — not by a disease name alone.

What are the ingredients of Gui Zhi Jia Hou Po Xing Zi Tang?

Gui Zhi Jia Hou Po Xing Zi Tang (桂枝加厚朴杏子汤) contains 7 herbs: Gui Zhi (cinnamon twig) 9g; Bai Shao (white peony root) 9g; Zhi Gan Cao (honey-fried licorice) 6g; Sheng Jiang (fresh ginger) 9g; Da Zao (jujube) 12 pieces; Hou Po (magnolia bark) 6g; Xing Ren (apricot kernel) 6g.

Where does Gui Zhi Jia Hou Po Xing Zi Tang come from?

Shang Han Lun, line 18.
