Gui Zhi Jia Ge Gen Tang (桂枝加葛根汤): Ingredients, Uses & Original Text

Gui Zhi Jia Ge Gen Tang — 桂枝加葛根汤, the Cinnamon Twig Decoction with Pueraria — is a classical Chinese herbal formula of 6 herbs from the Shang Han Lun. It belongs to the Taiyang (Greater Yang) stage and is aimed at one pattern: taiyang wind-strike with stiffness and tension along the neck and upper back.

Ingredients of Gui Zhi Jia Ge Gen Tang

  • Ge Gen (kudzu root) — 12g
  • Gui Zhi (cinnamon twig) — 9g · bark removed
  • Bai Shao (white peony root) — 9g
  • Zhi Gan Cao (honey-fried licorice) — 6g
  • Sheng Jiang (fresh ginger) — 9g · sliced
  • Da Zao (jujube) — 12 pieces · split

Doses are typical modern textbook amounts for one day's decoction, shown for study. They are not a prescription.

What Gui Zhi Jia Ge Gen Tang is used for

Classical pattern: Taiyang wind-strike with stiffness and tension along the neck and upper back.

Typical pulse: floating, moderate pulse.

Modern clinical uses: Cold with sweating accompanied by stiff, aching neck and shoulders.

The original line

In Taiyang disease, where the neck and upper back are stiff and rigid, yet conversely there is perspiration and aversion to wind: Gui Zhi Jia Ge Gen Tang governs it.

太阳病，项背强几几，反汗出恶风者，桂枝加葛根汤主之。

— Shang Han Lun, line 14

How to prepare Gui Zhi Jia Ge Gen Tang

Decoct with water. Drink warm and induce a mild, gentle sweat.

Cautions

If sweating is absent, switch to Ge Gen Tang.

Questions about Gui Zhi Jia Ge Gen Tang

What is Gui Zhi Jia Ge Gen Tang used for?

In classical terms Gui Zhi Jia Ge Gen Tang treats taiyang wind-strike with stiffness and tension along the neck and upper back. Modern references apply it to cold with sweating accompanied by stiff, aching neck and shoulders. It should be chosen by pattern, by a qualified practitioner — not by a disease name alone.

What are the ingredients of Gui Zhi Jia Ge Gen Tang?

Gui Zhi Jia Ge Gen Tang (桂枝加葛根汤) contains 6 herbs: Ge Gen (kudzu root) 12g; Gui Zhi (cinnamon twig) 9g; Bai Shao (white peony root) 9g; Zhi Gan Cao (honey-fried licorice) 6g; Sheng Jiang (fresh ginger) 9g; Da Zao (jujube) 12 pieces.

Where does Gui Zhi Jia Ge Gen Tang come from?

Shang Han Lun, line 14.
