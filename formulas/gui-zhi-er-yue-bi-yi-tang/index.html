Gui Zhi Er Yue Bi Yi Tang (桂枝二越婢一汤): Ingredients, Uses & Original Text

Gui Zhi Er Yue Bi Yi Tang — 桂枝二越婢一汤, the Two-to-One Cinnamon Twig and Maidservant from Yue Decoction — is a classical Chinese herbal formula of 7 herbs from the Shang Han Lun. It belongs to the Taiyang (Greater Yang) stage and is aimed at one pattern: mild Taiyang exterior constraint accompanied by internal heat.

Ingredients of Gui Zhi Er Yue Bi Yi Tang

  • Gui Zhi (cinnamon twig) — 4.5g · bark removed
  • Bai Shao (white peony root) — 4.5g
  • Ma Huang (ephedra) — 4.5g · nodes removed
  • Zhi Gan Cao (honey-fried licorice) — 3g
  • Da Zao (jujube) — 4 pieces · split
  • Sheng Jiang (fresh ginger) — 4.5g · sliced
  • Shi Gao (gypsum) — 8g · crushed

Doses are typical modern textbook amounts for one day's decoction, shown for study. They are not a prescription.

What Gui Zhi Er Yue Bi Yi Tang is used for

Classical pattern: Mild Taiyang exterior constraint accompanied by internal heat.

Typical pulse: faint, weak pulse.

Modern clinical uses: Common cold with more fever than chills, mild vexation and thirst.

The original line

Gui Zhi Er Yue Bi Yi Tang is indicated.

宜桂枝二越婢一汤。

— Shang Han Lun, line 27

How to prepare Gui Zhi Er Yue Bi Yi Tang

Decoct Ma Huang first, remove foam, then decoct with remaining ingredients. Drink warm.

Cautions

Contraindicated in pure cold patterns. Predominant heat with mild aversion to cold is the key diagnostic factor.

Questions about Gui Zhi Er Yue Bi Yi Tang

What is Gui Zhi Er Yue Bi Yi Tang used for?

In classical terms Gui Zhi Er Yue Bi Yi Tang treats mild Taiyang exterior constraint accompanied by internal heat. Modern references apply it to common cold with more fever than chills, mild vexation and thirst. It should be chosen by pattern, by a qualified practitioner — not by a disease name alone.

What are the ingredients of Gui Zhi Er Yue Bi Yi Tang?

Gui Zhi Er Yue Bi Yi Tang (桂枝二越婢一汤) contains 7 herbs: Gui Zhi (cinnamon twig) 4.5g; Bai Shao (white peony root) 4.5g; Ma Huang (ephedra) 4.5g; Zhi Gan Cao (honey-fried licorice) 3g; Da Zao (jujube) 4 pieces; Sheng Jiang (fresh ginger) 4.5g; Shi Gao (gypsum) 8g.

Where does Gui Zhi Er Yue Bi Yi Tang come from?
