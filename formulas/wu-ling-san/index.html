Wu Ling San (五苓散): Ingredients, Uses & Original Text

Wu Ling San — 五苓散, the Five-Ingredient Powder with Poria — is a classical Chinese herbal formula of 5 herbs from the Shang Han Lun. It belongs to the Taiyang (Greater Yang) stage and is aimed at one pattern: taiyang water accumulation, dysfunction of bladder qi transformation.

Ingredients of Wu Ling San

  • Zhu Ling (polyporus sclerotium) — 9g
  • Ze Xie (alisma rhizome) — 15g
  • Bai Zhu (white atractylodes rhizome) — 9g
  • Fu Ling (poria fungus) — 9g
  • Gui Zhi (cinnamon twig) — 6g · bark removed

Doses are typical modern textbook amounts for one day's decoction, shown for study. They are not a prescription.

What Wu Ling San is used for

Classical pattern: Taiyang water accumulation, dysfunction of bladder qi transformation.

Typical pulse: floating pulse.

Modern clinical uses: Acute gastroenteritis, edema, cystitis, hangover, water-retention vertigo.

The original line

With a floating pulse, dysuria, mild heat, and thirst: Wu Ling San governs it.

脉浮，小便不利，微热消渴者，五苓散主之。

— Shang Han Lun, line 71

How to prepare Wu Ling San

Decoct with water and drink plenty of warm water after administration to promote mild sweat and urination.

Cautions

Use with caution in thirst caused by yin deficiency and fluid exhaustion.

Questions about Wu Ling San

What is Wu Ling San used for?

In classical terms Wu Ling San treats taiyang water accumulation, dysfunction of bladder qi transformation. Modern references apply it to acute gastroenteritis, edema, cystitis, hangover, water-retention vertigo. It should be chosen by pattern, by a qualified practitioner — not by a disease name alone.

What are the ingredients of Wu Ling San?

Wu Ling San (五苓散) contains 5 herbs: Zhu Ling (polyporus sclerotium) 9g; Ze Xie (alisma rhizome) 15g; Bai Zhu (white atractylodes rhizome) 9g; Fu Ling (poria fungus) 9g; Gui Zhi (cinnamon twig) 6g.

Where does Wu Ling San come from?

Shang Han Lun, line 71.
