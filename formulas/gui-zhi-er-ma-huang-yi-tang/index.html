Gui Zhi Er Ma Huang Yi Tang (桂枝二麻黄一汤): Ingredients, Uses & Original Text

Gui Zhi Er Ma Huang Yi Tang — 桂枝二麻黄一汤, the Two-to-One Cinnamon Twig and Ephedra Decoction — is a classical Chinese herbal formula of 7 herbs from the Shang Han Lun. It belongs to the Taiyang (Greater Yang) stage and is aimed at one pattern: very mild Taiyang pattern, residual exterior pathogen lingering after sweating.

Ingredients of Gui Zhi Er Ma Huang Yi Tang

  • Gui Zhi (cinnamon twig) — 5g · bark removed
  • Bai Shao (white peony root) — 5g
  • Ma Huang (ephedra) — 2g · nodes removed
  • Sheng Jiang (fresh ginger) — 4g · sliced
  • Xing Ren (apricot kernel) — 4g · skin and tip removed
  • Zhi Gan Cao (honey-fried licorice) — 3g
  • Da Zao (jujube) — 5 pieces · split

Doses are typical modern textbook amounts for one day's decoction, shown for study. They are not a prescription.

What Gui Zhi Er Ma Huang Yi Tang is used for

Classical pattern: Very mild Taiyang pattern, residual exterior pathogen lingering after sweating.

Typical pulse: flooding, large pulse.

Modern clinical uses: Very mild cold, persistent unresolved low fever after diaphoresis.

The original line

Gui Zhi Er Ma Huang Yi Tang is indicated.

宜桂枝二麻黄一汤。

— Shang Han Lun, line 25

How to prepare Gui Zhi Er Ma Huang Yi Tang

Decoct Ma Huang first, skim off froth, add other ingredients, and take warm.

Cautions

Has very gentle diaphoretic action; only suited for extremely mild residual patterns.

Questions about Gui Zhi Er Ma Huang Yi Tang

What is Gui Zhi Er Ma Huang Yi Tang used for?

In classical terms Gui Zhi Er Ma Huang Yi Tang treats very mild Taiyang pattern, residual exterior pathogen lingering after sweating. Modern references apply it to very mild cold, persistent unresolved low fever after diaphoresis. It should be chosen by pattern, by a qualified practitioner — not by a disease name alone.

What are the ingredients of Gui Zhi Er Ma Huang Yi Tang?

Gui Zhi Er Ma Huang Yi Tang (桂枝二麻黄一汤) contains 7 herbs: Gui Zhi (cinnamon twig) 5g; Bai Shao (white peony root) 5g; Ma Huang (ephedra) 2g; Sheng Jiang (fresh ginger) 4g; Xing Ren (apricot kernel) 4g; Zhi Gan Cao (honey-fried licorice) 3g; Da Zao (jujube) 5 pieces.
