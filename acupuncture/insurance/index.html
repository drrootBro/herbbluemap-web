Is Acupuncture Covered by Insurance?

Short answer: often, but rarely in full and rarely for everything. Coverage depends on your plan, the condition being treated, and who does the needling. Here is how it usually works in the US, and the five questions that save you a surprise bill.

Medicare

Since 2020, Original Medicare (Part B) covers acupuncture for chronic low back pain: pain lasting 12 weeks or longer with no identifiable systemic cause such as cancer or infection, and not related to surgery or pregnancy. It covers up to 12 sessions in 90 days, and 8 more if you are improving, up to 20 a year. One catch: Medicare does not let licensed acupuncturists bill it on their own. The acupuncturist must work under the supervision of a physician or other qualifying provider who bills Medicare. Medicare Advantage plans may cover more, so check your plan's benefits.

Private and employer plans

Many commercial plans now include acupuncture, usually with limits: a set number of visits a year, a copay per visit, and coverage only for certain conditions, most often pain and nausea. Some plans require a referral or pre-authorization. If your acupuncturist is out of network, ask for a superbill (an itemized receipt with diagnosis and procedure codes) to submit for reimbursement.

HSA and FSA

Acupuncture is a qualified medical expense under IRS rules, so you can pay with a Health Savings Account or Flexible Spending Account card even when your insurance does not cover it. Herbs prescribed for a specific condition may qualify too; ask your plan administrator.

Medicaid and workers' comp

Medicaid coverage of acupuncture depends on the state and changes over time, so check your state's current benefits. Workers' compensation and no-fault auto insurance often cover acupuncture for injury-related pain when it is part of the treatment plan.

Five questions to ask before you book

  1. Is acupuncture a covered benefit on my plan, and for which conditions?
  2. How many visits a year, and what is my copay or coinsurance?
  3. Do I need a referral or pre-authorization?
  4. Is this acupuncturist in network? If not, will they give me a superbill?
  5. Does the clinic accept HSA/FSA cards?

Questions, answered straight

Does Medicare cover acupuncture?

Yes, for chronic low back pain: up to 12 sessions in 90 days, plus 8 more if you improve, up to 20 a year. The acupuncturist must work under a provider who can bill Medicare.

Can I use my HSA or FSA for acupuncture?

Yes. Acupuncture is a qualified medical expense under IRS rules, so HSA and FSA funds can pay for it.

Does insurance cover acupuncture for anxiety or fertility?

Sometimes. Many plans limit coverage to pain and nausea. Ask your insurer whether your specific diagnosis is covered before you start.

What is a superbill?

An itemized receipt with diagnosis and procedure codes that you can send to your insurer to request out-of-network reimbursement.
