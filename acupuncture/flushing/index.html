How to check an acupuncturist's license in New York

New York licenses acupuncturists through the State Education Department's Office of the Professions. Anyone can look up a license number, its status and its registration date on the Office of the Professions online verification page. Look for a license that is current, a registration that has not lapsed, and no disciplinary actions. Every practitioner in our directory was checked this way, one by one, and anyone with a disciplinary record is left out.

What to expect at your first visit

A first appointment usually runs 60 to 90 minutes. Expect questions about your main complaint, sleep, digestion and medications; many practitioners also look at your tongue and take your pulse at both wrists (here is what they look for). Needles are hair-thin, sterile and single-use. Most people feel a brief pinch, then heaviness or warmth. Follow-ups are shorter, and for chronic problems a typical plan is weekly sessions for four to eight weeks before deciding whether it is working.

Cost and insurance

Ask each clinic for its first-visit and follow-up prices, whether it bills your insurance, and whether it takes HSA/FSA cards. Our insurance guide lists the questions to ask before booking.

Questions, answered straight

How do I find a good acupuncturist in Flushing?

Start with a verified license, then ask what conditions they treat most often, how many sessions they expect, and how you will measure progress. The list on this page only includes licenses checked against New York State records.

Do acupuncturists in Flushing take insurance?

Some do, especially for back pain and injury-related pain; many accept HSA and FSA cards. Call ahead and ask whether they are in network with your plan.
