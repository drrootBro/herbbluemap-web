How Much Does Acupuncture Cost?

Short answer: in most of the US, plan on roughly $75–$150 for a follow-up session, more for the first visit, and more again in Manhattan. Community clinics can be much cheaper, and insurance or an HSA can bring the real cost down. Here is how pricing usually works.

Typical prices per session

  • First visit: often longer (60–90 minutes) and priced higher, because it includes a full intake.
  • Follow-up session: roughly $75–$150 in many US cities.
  • Manhattan and other high-rent areas: often above that range; Queens practices, including Flushing, are frequently lower.
  • Community acupuncture: treatment in a shared room on a sliding scale, often far cheaper per visit.
  • Add-ons: cupping, gua sha, electro-acupuncture or herbs may cost extra.

These are rough ranges, not quotes. Always ask the clinic for its current prices before booking.

Cost with insurance

If your plan covers acupuncture, you usually pay a copay or coinsurance per visit, up to a yearly visit limit. Medicare covers it for chronic low back pain. Details are in our insurance guide.

Cost without insurance

Ask about package prices for a series of sessions, community or student clinics (acupuncture school clinics are supervised and cheaper), and whether the clinic accepts HSA/FSA cards — acupuncture is an eligible expense.

How many sessions will I need?

For a chronic problem, budget for about six sessions before deciding whether it works. A practitioner who cannot give you a rough plan and a way to measure progress is a warning sign.

Questions, answered straight

How much is an acupuncture session without insurance?

In many US cities, roughly $75–$150 for a follow-up session, with first visits costing more. Community clinics can be much cheaper.

How much does acupuncture cost in NYC?

Manhattan prices are often above the national range; Queens, including Flushing, is frequently lower. Ask each clinic for current prices.

Can I use my HSA for acupuncture?

Yes. Acupuncture is a qualified medical expense, so HSA and FSA funds can pay for it.

Do you tip an acupuncturist?

Tipping is not expected in a medical setting. Some spa-style clinics accept tips, but a licensed acupuncturist will not expect one.
