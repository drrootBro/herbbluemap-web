Acupuncture for Anxiety and Stress: What It Can and Can't Do

Many people come to acupuncture for pain and notice they sleep better and feel calmer. Others come for anxiety directly. The research is younger than for pain, but encouraging, and the treatment itself is one of the gentlest things you can try.

Does acupuncture help with anxiety?

Small and medium-sized trials report lower anxiety scores after a course of acupuncture, with few side effects. The studies vary in quality, so the honest summary is: promising, worth trying alongside proven treatments, not a replacement for them. Acupuncture appears to calm the body's stress response — slower heart rate, more parasympathetic ("rest and digest") activity — which is also how most people describe the feeling on the table.

Which points are used?

Classic calming points include Yintang between the eyebrows, HT7 at the wrist crease, PC6 on the inner forearm, and points on the ear (the NADA protocol, used widely in US addiction and trauma clinics). In Chinese medicine terms, anxiety often maps to a heart–kidney or liver-qi pattern, and the point choice follows the pattern.

What a session feels like

Most people feel heavy and drowsy within ten minutes and many fall asleep. A course is usually weekly for six to eight weeks.

Questions, answered straight

Can acupuncture cure anxiety?

No treatment 'cures' anxiety on its own, but studies suggest acupuncture can lower anxiety symptoms, and it pairs well with therapy and medication.

Where do the needles go for anxiety?

Common points are between the eyebrows, at the wrist, on the inner forearm, and on the ear. Your practitioner chooses based on your pattern.

How many acupuncture sessions for anxiety?

Usually weekly for six to eight weeks, then less often if it helps.
