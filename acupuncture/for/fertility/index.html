Acupuncture for Fertility and IVF: What It Can and Can't Do

Fertility acupuncture is one of the fastest-growing reasons Americans see an acupuncturist, often alongside IVF. It is also an area where honest information matters, because hope is expensive.

Does acupuncture help fertility?

The evidence is mixed. Early studies suggested acupuncture around embryo transfer raised IVF pregnancy rates; larger, better-designed trials since then mostly found no clear difference in live births. What studies more consistently show is lower stress and anxiety during treatment, which many patients value for its own sake. Any acupuncturist who promises higher pregnancy rates is overselling.

How it is usually done

Typical plans are weekly sessions for two to three months before a cycle or transfer, sometimes with sessions on the day of embryo transfer. Chinese medicine practitioners may add herbs; if you are in an IVF cycle, only take herbs with your fertility clinic's knowledge, because some can affect hormones or bleeding.

For men too

A few studies look at sperm quality with acupuncture; results are preliminary. Fertility is a two-person workup, so make sure both partners have been evaluated medically.

Cost

Fertility acupuncture is rarely covered by insurance, but it is an HSA/FSA-eligible expense. See acupuncture cost.

See a fertility specialist if you are under 35 and have been trying for a year, over 35 and trying for six months, or have irregular periods, known endometriosis or prior pelvic infection.

Questions, answered straight

Does acupuncture actually work for fertility?

The best recent trials do not show a clear increase in IVF live-birth rates, but acupuncture can reduce stress during treatment. Be wary of anyone promising results.

When should I start fertility acupuncture?

Many practitioners suggest two to three months before an IVF cycle or transfer, weekly.

Is fertility acupuncture covered by insurance?

Rarely, but you can usually pay with HSA or FSA funds.
