Acupuncture for Neuropathy and Nerve Pain: What It Can and Can't Do

Peripheral neuropathy — numbness, tingling, burning or electric pain, usually starting in the feet — affects millions of Americans, most often from diabetes or chemotherapy. Medication helps some people only partly, which is why many ask about acupuncture.

Does acupuncture help neuropathy?

Several trials and reviews report improvement in symptoms and, in some studies, nerve conduction, for diabetic and chemotherapy-induced neuropathy. Study sizes are modest and results vary, so treat it as a reasonable thing to try with your doctor's knowledge, not a guaranteed fix. Major US cancer centers offer acupuncture in their integrative medicine programs, including for chemotherapy side effects.

How it is done

Needles are placed on the legs and feet (for example ST36 below the knee, and the Bafeng points between the toes), often with electro-acupuncture. A typical course is one or two sessions a week for eight to twelve weeks.

Safety with diabetes

People with diabetes heal more slowly and may not feel a small injury on numb feet. Make sure your acupuncturist uses sterile single-use needles, cleans the skin, and checks your feet. Do not needle feet with open sores or poor circulation.

See a doctor for new numbness or weakness, symptoms that spread quickly, or any foot wound that is not healing. Neuropathy has many causes, including B12 deficiency and thyroid disease, that are worth ruling out.

Questions, answered straight

Can acupuncture reverse neuropathy?

It is not proven to reverse nerve damage, but several studies report fewer symptoms and some improvement in nerve function.

How many acupuncture sessions for neuropathy?

Often one or two a week for eight to twelve weeks before judging the result.

Is acupuncture safe for diabetic feet?

Generally yes with sterile technique, but avoid needling skin with sores or very poor circulation, and tell your practitioner you have diabetes.
