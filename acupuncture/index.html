Acupuncture: What It Is, What It Helps, and How to Find a Good Acupuncturist

Acupuncture is the practice of inserting very thin, sterile needles at specific points on the body. It comes from traditional Chinese medicine, where the points sit on channels that map how the body's systems connect. In the US today it is offered by licensed acupuncturists, and by some physicians, chiropractors and physical therapists with extra training. Millions of Americans use it every year, most often for pain.

What acupuncture is used for

The strongest research support is for chronic pain. The American College of Physicians lists acupuncture among first-line non-drug treatments for chronic low back pain, and reviews summarized by the National Institutes of Health find it can help neck pain, knee osteoarthritis pain, tension headaches and migraine prevention. People also seek it for insomnia, anxiety and stress, nausea, digestive complaints, allergies, menstrual pain and fertility support. The evidence for those is more mixed, and a good practitioner will tell you so.

How it works — two ways to say it

The classical way: qi and blood flow through channels; pain and illness come from blockage or imbalance; needling specific points restores the flow. The research way: needling stimulates nerves, muscle and connective tissue, changes how the brain processes pain signals, and triggers the release of the body's own pain-relieving chemicals. You do not have to believe the first to benefit from the second.

Is acupuncture safe?

In the hands of a licensed practitioner, serious problems are rare. Needles sold in the US are regulated by the FDA as medical devices and must be sterile and single-use. Common minor effects are small bruises, brief soreness or feeling tired afterward. Tell your practitioner if you are pregnant, have a pacemaker, take blood thinners or have a bleeding disorder.

What to expect at your first visit

A first appointment usually runs 60 to 90 minutes. Expect questions about your main complaint, sleep, digestion and medications; many practitioners also look at your tongue and take your pulse at both wrists (here is what they look for). Needles are hair-thin, sterile and single-use. Most people feel a brief pinch, then heaviness or warmth. Follow-ups are shorter, and for chronic problems a typical plan is weekly sessions for four to eight weeks before deciding whether it is working.

How much does acupuncture cost?

Prices vary a lot by city. Many US clinics charge roughly $75–$150 for a follow-up session, with first visits costing more. More detail: how much acupuncture costs. Manhattan and other high-rent areas often charge more, while community acupuncture clinics, where several patients are treated in one room, can cost much less. Ask about package prices, and whether the clinic gives you a receipt you can submit to insurance or your HSA.

Does insurance cover acupuncture?

Often, partly. Medicare covers acupuncture for chronic low back pain. Many private and employer plans cover a set number of visits, and acupuncture is an eligible expense for HSA and FSA accounts. Read our full guide: Is acupuncture covered by insurance?

Questions, answered straight

Does acupuncture hurt?

Usually very little. The needles are much thinner than injection needles. Most people feel a quick pinch, then a dull ache, heaviness or warmth at the point, which practitioners consider a sign the point is working.

How many acupuncture sessions do I need?

For a recent problem, a few sessions may be enough. For chronic pain, a common plan is once a week for four to eight weeks, then reassess. If nothing has changed after five or six sessions, it is reasonable to stop.

Is acupuncture covered by insurance?

Often partly. Medicare covers it for chronic low back pain, many private plans cover a limited number of visits, and it is an HSA/FSA-eligible expense. See our insurance guide.

What is the difference between acupuncture and dry needling?

They use similar needles. Dry needling is done by physical therapists and others, targets trigger points in tight muscles, and is based on Western anatomy only. Acupuncture is done by licensed acupuncturists, uses the full system of points and channels, and treats more than muscle pain.

How do I find a good acupuncturist?

Check the license with your state board, ask what conditions they treat most, and ask how they will measure progress. In New York you can start with our verified directory.
