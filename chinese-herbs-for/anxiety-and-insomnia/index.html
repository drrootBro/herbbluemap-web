Chinese Herbs for Anxiety and Insomnia: What Chinese Medicine Uses and Why

In Chinese medicine, anxiety and insomnia are usually read together: a mind that cannot settle by day will not settle by night. The classical texts describe several distinct patterns, and the herbs follow the pattern rather than the diagnosis.

Restless, can't fall asleep, tired but wired

Often read as blood and yin not nourishing the heart. The best-known formula is Suan Zao Ren Tang (sour jujube seed decoction), from the Jin Gui Yao Lue, built around sour jujube seed with poria, zhi mu, chuan xiong and licorice.

Weepy, sighing, emotionally fragile

The tiny three-ingredient Gan Mai Da Zao Tang — licorice, wheat and jujube — written for "restless organ" emotional distress. It is food-grade, which is part of why it is still popular.

Palpitations and a racing heart

The Shang Han Lun treats palpitations after over-sweating with Gui Zhi Gan Cao Tang, and a knotted, irregular pulse with Zhi Gan Cao Tang. Palpitations always need a medical check first.

Stress, irritability, tight chest

Classically "liver qi constraint", treated with chai hu (bupleurum)-based formulas. Schisandra is the traditional calming tonic.

Pair with acupuncture

Many people combine herbs with acupuncture for anxiety, which has gentle, well-tolerated effects on the stress response.

Before you take Chinese herbs: herbs are medicine. They can interact with prescription drugs (blood thinners, blood-pressure, diabetes and psychiatric medications especially), and quality varies by supplier. Avoid them in pregnancy unless a qualified practitioner prescribes them, buy from suppliers that test for heavy metals, and tell your doctor what you take.
Important: herbs can add to or interfere with antidepressants, anti-anxiety drugs and sleep medication — never stop or combine them without your prescriber. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) now.

Questions, answered straight

What is the best Chinese herb for sleep?

Sour jujube seed (suan zao ren) is the classic sleep herb, usually given in the formula Suan Zao Ren Tang. Choice depends on the pattern, so see a practitioner.

Are Chinese herbs for anxiety safe with SSRIs?

Some herbs interact with psychiatric medications. Always check with your prescriber and a qualified practitioner before combining them.

Can Chinese medicine help insomnia?

Many people report better sleep with herbs and acupuncture, and some studies are encouraging, but evidence is mixed. Good sleep habits come first.
